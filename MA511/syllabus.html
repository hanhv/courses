Syllabus

Linear Algebra and its Applications — Fall 2026

Basic Information
  • Instructor: Hanh Vo.
  • The class meets MWF 2:30 PM – 3:20 PM, CA 347.
  • Office Hours: MWF 3:45 PM – 4:45 PM, ET 314F.
Course Content
  • Text Book: Linear Algebra and its Applications, 4th Edition, by Gilbert Strang. We will cover most of the first 6 chapters of the book.
  • Brightspace: This is where you will find announcements and course documents.
Grades

Course grades will be determined from your overall score computed as follows:

Assessment Percentage
Quiz 30%
Midterm Exam 1 20%
Midterm Exam 2 20%
Final Exam 30%
Students who earn at least 97% of the total points in this course are guaranteed an A+, 93% guarantees an A, 90% an A-, 87% a B+, 83% a B, 80% a B-, 77% a C+, 73% a C, 70% a C-, 67% a D+, 63% a D, and 60% a D-.

For each of these grades, it is possible that a somewhat lower percentage will be sufficient to earn that grade at the end of the semester.
Quizzes and Practice Problems
  • In-Class Quizzes (30%): There will be regular in-class quizzes throughout the semester, typically administered on Fridays, except during exam weeks. These quizzes will be similar to the examples covered in lectures and the assigned practice problems. At the end of the semester, the two lowest quiz scores will be dropped. No late or make-up quizzes will be allowed without prior documented approval.
  • Practice Problems: To help you prepare for the quizzes and exams, recommended problem sets will be assigned regularly. These problem sets will not be collected or graded. However, completing and understanding these problems is essential for success on the quizzes and examinations.
Examinations

There will be two midterm exams and a final exam. Please see the course schedule for the midterm exam dates.

Details about the final exam will be announced later in the semester by the registrar's office.

Grade Appeals

Regrade requests will only be accepted for 24 hours after the grades are returned.

Calculators

Calculators and other electronic devices will not be allowed for use during in-class quizzes, midterms, or the final exam.

Collaboration and Academic Integrity

Collaboration on ungraded practice problems is allowed and highly encouraged. However, all graded assessments (quizzes and exams) must be completed independently.

Use of Artificial Intelligence

Students may use learning resources, including AI tools, when working on the ungraded practice problems to aid their understanding. However, the use of AI tools, personal notes, or any other unauthorized resources is strictly prohibited during all graded assessments, including in-class quizzes and exams.

Make-up Policy

Except in unusual circumstances with official approval, no make-up quizzes or exams will be given. Students who need to miss a quiz or exam due to an emergent medical circumstance, grief, jury duty, military training, or another situation covered by the Office of the Dean of Students (ODOS) should submit an absence notification through the Student Absence Notification System:

https://www.purdue.edu/odos/support/students/absences.php

Please send the ODOS approval to Hanh Vo as soon as possible, preferably before the scheduled quiz or exam.

Academic Dishonesty

Academic integrity is one of the highest values that Purdue University holds. Individuals are encouraged to alert university officials to potential breaches of this value by either emailing integrity@purdue.edu or by calling 765-494-8778. While information may be submitted anonymously, the more information that is submitted provides the greatest opportunity for the university to investigate the concern.

Purdue prohibits “dishonesty in connection with any University activity. Cheating, plagiarism, or knowingly furnishing false information to the University are examples of dishonesty.” [Part 5, Section III-B-2-a, University Regulations] Furthermore, the University Senate has stipulated that “the commitment of acts of cheating, lying, and deceit in any of their diverse forms (such as the use of substitutes for taking examinations, the use of illegal cribs, plagiarism, and copying during examinations) is dishonest and must not be tolerated. Moreover, knowingly to aid and abet, directly or indirectly, other parties in committing dishonest acts is in itself dishonest.” [University Senate Document 72-18, December 15, 1972]. For more details about the Purdue Policy on academic dishonesty see: http://www.purdue.edu/odos/academic-integrity/
